If the Moon vanished right now, you'd still see it for 1.3 seconds.
That's how long its light takes to get here. After that, things go wrong fast. And the strangest part? It's already happening.
First, the tides. The Sun pulls on our oceans too, but only about half as hard. So high tides would shrink to a fraction of today's. Surfers: devastated.
But that's the easy part.
Every night would be as dark as a new moon. No full moon. Ever again. Werewolves? Unemployed.
Animals would lose their calendar. Corals on the Great Barrier Reef time their mass spawning with the Moon. No Moon, no signal.
Now it gets weirder, because the Moon also controls time.
Its pull is slowly braking Earth's spin. 1.4 billion years ago, a day lasted about 18 hours. A Monday six hours shorter? Honestly, sounds great.
Without the Moon, our days would almost stop getting longer.
Then there's the scary one. The Moon keeps Earth's tilt steady. Without it, the tilt could wobble far more over millions of years. Seasons would become... suggestions.
And that strange part I promised? The Moon is leaving us. Lasers we've bounced off it since 1969 show it drifts about 3.8 centimeters farther away every year. Roughly as fast as your fingernails grow.
