An asteroid is heading for Earth. And you're the one who has to stop it. And you, who are very smart… decide to blow it up with a nuke. It doesn't work. Because now, instead of one giant rock, you've got a thousand smaller rocks… and they're all still coming. And you, who are very smart… land on it and try to push it away with rockets. It doesn't work either. Because the asteroid is spinning like a washing machine. Your rockets push in every direction… and it goes nowhere. And you, who are very smart… paint one side white, so sunlight pushes it off course. It works… but way too slowly. Because sunlight is so weak, it would take about fifty years. And you don't have fifty years. And you, who are very smart… park a spaceship right next to it, so its gravity gently pulls the asteroid away. Same problem. Because that pull is tiny. Decades again. Then you realize: the secret isn't pushing harder. It's pushing earlier. So you crash a small spaceship into it, years before it arrives. Because a tiny nudge out in space grows into thousands of kilometers by the time it reaches Earth. And it's real. In 2022, NASA did exactly this with a spacecraft called DART… and changed an asteroid's orbit. That one made me scratch my head. Which means, next time an asteroid is heading for Earth…
